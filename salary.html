    How the savings figure is worked out

    The UAE and Kuwait do not tax salaries, so the calculation is simple: salary minus monthly costs. What is left is converted to rupees at the rate shown, which you can edit.

    If the employer provides housing or transport, tick the box and that cost is removed. Many Gulf offers quote a single figure that already includes a housing allowance, so check your offer letter before ticking it.

    How the India comparison works

    A Gulf salary and an Indian CTC cannot be compared directly, because one is tax-free with high rent and the other is taxed with lower costs. This calculator compares what you keep.

    • It takes your Gulf savings in rupees.
    • It adds your monthly living cost in India to get the in-hand pay you would need there.
    • It adds Indian income tax using the FY 2025-26 new-regime slabs, with the ₹75,000 standard deduction and 4% cess.

    The result is a gross salary. A CTC on an Indian offer letter is usually a little higher, because it also counts employer PF and gratuity.

    Costs people forget

    • Visa, medical and Emirates ID or civil ID costs if the employer does not cover them.
    • Flights home, and whether the contract includes an annual ticket.
    • Rent deposits and agency fees when you first arrive.
    • Money transfer fees and the exchange-rate margin on remittances.
    • School fees, if family will join you.
